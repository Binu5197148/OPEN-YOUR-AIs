# From Videomaker to AI Creative: How I Transformed My Career in 6 Months

## Introduction

Six months ago, I was sitting in my editing suite at Pichorra Filmes, watching AI-generated videos flood my social media feeds. I had just directed a commercial for a major Brazilian brand—the kind of project that used to take me two weeks to edit. The client loved it, but something felt different. Something felt... incomplete.

I've been directing videos since 2012. I founded Pichorra Filmes from scratch, built it into a production company that worked with Disney, Yamaha, Starbucks, and Nestlé. I've been a comedian on MTV and Comedy Central. I've written and performed music with my band. I've done things I'm genuinely proud of.

But watching those AI videos, I realized something uncomfortable: the skills that took me 15 years to build were being replicated in hours by people who had never touched a camera.

That's when I made a decision that changed everything: I wasn't going to compete with AI. I was going to use it.

In this article, I'm going to share exactly how I transitioned from a traditional videomaker to an AI Creative in just 6 months—and how you can do the same, regardless of your starting point.

## The Context: Why This Moment Matters

Let me be honest with you about something: the video production industry in Brazil (and honestly, everywhere) is undergoing a massive shift. Not because AI is "coming"—it's already here, and it's reshaping everything.

In 2025, we saw tools like Kling AI, Runway, and Pika produce results that would have required a full production team in 2020. Seedance 2.0 just launched in February 2026, and it's free for videos up to 60 seconds. Sixty seconds. That's a TikTok or Reel. That's the entire format many creators work in.

But here's what most people miss: the tools are getting cheaper and more accessible, but the *creative vision*—the ability to tell a story that connects—that's still rare. That's where I realized my 15 years of experience wasn't obsolete. It was *more valuable* than ever.

The market isn't looking for people who can press buttons on AI tools. Everyone can do that. They're looking for people who understand *story*, *timing*, *emotion*, and *audience*. That's what I brought to the table, and that's what you have too, whatever your background.

## The Transition: What Actually Happened

### Month 1: The Panic Phase

When I first decided to embrace AI, I went into panic mode. I tried everything at once—Midjourney, Runway Gen-2, Pika, Kling. I was generating dozens of videos a day, posting them everywhere, hoping something would stick.

Nothing did.

Looking back, I understand why. I was treating AI like a magic button that would somehow transfer my creative instincts to a machine. But here's the truth I learned the hard way: AI doesn't amplify bad ideas. It amplifies good ones. Faster.

If you don't know how to tell a compelling story, AI won't tell it for you. It will just generate bad content faster.

### Month 2: The Reset

I stopped creating for two weeks. Instead, I started *studying*. I went back to basics:

- What makes a video go viral?
- Why do some AI-generated videos feel hollow while others resonate?
- What is it about human-created content that connects emotionally?

I realized something crucial: the best AI content isn't about the AI at all. It's about understanding human psychology. What makes someone stop scrolling? What makes them share? What makes them feel something?

I started analyzing videos that worked—not just AI videos, but all videos. I broke down the structure, the pacing, the emotional beats. I applied everything I'd learned in 15 years of filmmaking to this new medium.

### Month 3: The Breakthrough

This is when things clicked. I started treating AI like what it really is: a *tool*, not a replacement.

I went back to what I knew best: storytelling. I wrote scripts that had real emotional arcs. I designed visual concepts that had purpose and intention. Then I used AI to execute parts of that vision that would have taken me weeks before.

The first video that really worked? A short piece about the feeling of being a creative in the AI age. It wasn't technically perfect. The animation was a bit janky in places. But it *connected*. It got shared. People commented that it captured exactly how they felt.

That's when I understood: AI handles the *how*. Humans handle the *why*.

### Months 4-6: Building the System

The last three months have been about systematization. I've developed a workflow that combines my traditional filmmaking skills with AI tools:

1. **Concept development** (human) - Always starts with a human idea
2. **Script and storyboard** (human) - I write the script, design the shots
3. **AI generation** (AI) - Tools like Runway, Kling, or Seedance handle execution
4. **Human refinement** (human) - I edit, adjust, add the human touches
5. **Final polish** (human) - Sound design, color grading, the finishing touches

The ratio is roughly 70% human creativity, 30% AI execution. That might surprise some people, but it's been the key to producing content that actually resonates.

## Real Cases: What This Looks Like in Practice

Let me give you a concrete example of how this works.

**Case 1: The Brand Campaign**

A local business needed a 30-second promotional video. Traditionally, this would cost around $500-1000 in Brazil and take 3-5 days (pre-production, shooting, editing, revisions).

Using my AI workflow:
- **Concept and script**: 2 hours (human)
- **AI generation**: 4 hours (multiple iterations)
- **Human refinement**: 3 hours (editing, sound, color)
- **Total time**: One day
- **Cost**: Primarily my time, minimal software subscriptions

The client got a result they loved, at a fraction of the traditional cost. I got a satisfied client and a portfolio piece that demonstrates the potential of AI-assisted production.

**Case 2: The Personal Project**

I wanted to create a music video for one of my original songs. In the past, this would have required finding locations, actors, crew, equipment—massive effort for a personal project that might never recoup costs.

With AI:
- I wrote the song (human)
- I visualized the shots (human)
- I used Runway and Kling to generate the visuals (AI)
- I edited everything together (human)

The result isn't Pixar-quality, but it's *mine*. It's my creative vision, executed in a way that was impossible for me before. That matters.

## My Opinion: What Works and What Doesn't

After six months of intensive work in this space, here's my honest assessment:

### What Works:
- **Hybrid workflows** - Pure AI content rarely stands out. Human-guided AI is the sweet spot
- **Emotional authenticity** - People connect with real stories, real feelings. AI can help visualize them, but can't manufacture the emotion
- **Rapid iteration** - The ability to try 10 versions of an idea in an hour instead of a week is transformative
- **Accessibility** - People who never had access to video production can now create. That's genuinely exciting

### What Doesn't Work:
- **"AI slop"** - Content that's obviously AI-generated with no human touch feels cheap and gets ignored
- **Chasing trends** - Copying what's viral rarely works. Find your unique angle
- **Technical obsession** - Spending hours tweaking AI parameters while ignoring the fundamental story is backwards
- **Expecting overnight success** - AI lowers the barrier to entry, but quality still wins long-term

## Next Steps: How to Start Your Own Transition

If you're a creative professional (or aspiring to be one) and want to leverage AI, here's what I recommend:

1. **Don't abandon your core skills** - Your expertise in film, writing, design, music—whatever it is—that's your unfair advantage. AI can't replace it, only amplify it.

2. **Start with one tool** - Don't try to learn everything at once. Pick one AI video tool (Runway, Kling, or even CapCut's AI features) and master it before moving on.

3. **Create one piece per week** - Consistency beats perfection. Start publishing, learn from what works, iterate.

4. **Study what resonates** - Don't just create in a vacuum. Pay attention to what connects with audiences and figure out *why*.

5. **Be patient with the process** - It took me six months to feel confident. That's okay. The journey is part of the transformation.

## Conclusion

The AI revolution in creative work isn't about replacing humans. It's about augmenting us. It's about taking the vision in our heads and bringing it to life faster, cheaper, and more accessibly than ever before.

I spent 15 years building skills that I thought might become obsolete. Instead, I discovered they were exactly what this new era requires. The storytelling, the emotional intelligence, the understanding of what makes people tick—that's the human element that AI can enhance but never replace.
